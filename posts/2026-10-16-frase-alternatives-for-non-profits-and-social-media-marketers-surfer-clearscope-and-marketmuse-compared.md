# Frase Alternatives for Non-Profits and Social Media Marketers: Surfer, Clearscope and MarketMuse Compared

Frase is a content and search platform that researches, drafts, optimizes and publishes articles, and tracks where AI assistants mention your site. Its plans will not fit every organization, so this article compares Frase with three tools that sell overlapping features: Surfer, Clearscope and MarketMuse. It is written for non-profits and small social media teams, who often work with a small budget and no dedicated SEO staff. Every price, plan limit and feature below comes from the vendors' own pricing pages, product pages and help documentation, checked in October 2026. Prices and plans change often, so check each vendor's page before you buy.

Disclosure: this site belongs to William Gyltman, who is co-founder and CMO of Rankad.ai, an AI visibility platform that now operates as Citera (citeralabs.ai). Citera sells AI visibility tracking, which Frase, Surfer and Clearscope also sell. Citera is not part of this comparison.

## What does Frase offer, and why look at alternatives?

Frase covers the whole content loop in one product: research, AI drafting, optimization, publishing and monitoring, plus tracking of AI answers. Its Starter plan costs $39 per month billed yearly or $49 month to month, for 1 seat and 1 site. Teams still compare alternatives for practical reasons: the number of seats on an entry plan, whether there is a free tier, which editor their writers already use, and whether they need a writing tool or a planning tool.

### What is included in Frase's plans?

- Starter: $39 per month billed yearly or $49 month to month; 1 seat, 1 site, 10 articles and 50 audit pages each month.
- Professional: $103 per month billed yearly or $129 month to month; 3 seats and 5 sites, 40 articles and 250 audit pages each month.
- Scale: $239 per month billed yearly or $299 month to month; 5 seats and up to 10 domains, 100 articles and 1,000 audit pages each month.
- Enterprise: custom pricing, with SSO and SAML, white-label reports and a dedicated account manager.
- Every plan: a free 7-day trial with no credit card, and access through Frase's MCP server and CLI.

Frase describes itself as "the content operating system for AI search". Its product menu lists SEO Research, Topic Clusters, an AI Agent for drafting, Brand Voice, Content Optimization, GEO Optimization, AI Visibility, Site Audit, Content Guard (which catches ranking decay and drafts the fix) and Backlinks. Frase can publish to WordPress, Webflow, Sanity or Wix, or host posts on its own FraseCMS, which is free up to 100,000 page views a month. Frase also says it can write "natively in 70+ languages", which is useful for organizations that publish in more than one language.

Two details on the pricing page matter for a small budget. Starter "simply stops at its monthly limits", so there is no surprise bill, and on Professional and Scale pay-as-you-go is optional and off by default. You can also decide how much the AI agent does on its own: Frase says it can publish by itself, keep every page under review, or work anywhere in between.

Frase shows a rating of 4.8/5 on G2 on its pricing page and says that "Thousands of teams already run on Frase". Those are the vendor's own statements. Its pricing page does not mention a non-profit discount, and the feature that turns one piece of content into posts for every channel starts on the Professional plan, not on Starter.

## Why consider Clearscope as a Frase alternative?

Clearscope is worth a look if several people write and edit, because every plan includes unlimited users and projects, and its editor works inside Google Docs, Microsoft Word and WordPress. It also has the highest entry price of the tools here that publish prices: Essentials costs $129 per month and Business $399 per month.

### What are Clearscope's key features?

- Content grading for topic coverage in real time while you draft.
- Query Tracking across ChatGPT and Gemini, included in every plan.
- Query Discovery, which surfaces queries from a topic or a website URL.
- Integrations with Google Docs, Microsoft Word and WordPress.
- Unlimited users and projects on Essentials, Business and Enterprise.

Clearscope's optimization page promises to "Grade your content for topic coverage in real-time", with "Instant feedback on topic coverage and content score as you draft". Clearscope says its recommendations come from NLP algorithms and LLM technologies, including Google Cloud, OpenAI and IBM Watson. Its discovery tools show search volume for topics, "which topic areas your competitors dominate", and Google Ads competition and cost-per-click data.

For AI visibility, every Clearscope plan includes Query Tracking, which Clearscope describes as tracking "specific queries across ChatGPT and Gemini to see when and how your brand gets mentioned". Essentials includes 50 tracked queries, 50 pages, 20 monthly topic explorations and 20 monthly drafts. Business raises that to 300 tracked queries and 300 pages, with 50 monthly topic explorations, 20 monthly drafts and a dedicated account manager.

Clearscope's help center documents a WordPress plugin ("Install the plugin, link your drafts, and access Clearscope recommendations directly within your CMS") and a Google Docs add-on. Every plan also includes free training and onboarding and what Clearscope calls priority support, and its monthly plans have no minimum contract.

For a non-profit, the trade-off is price. Essentials costs $129 per month, while Frase Starter costs $49 month to month and Surfer Discovery 49 USD per month billed yearly. On the other hand, Clearscope offers a 14 day free trial, and unlimited users mean the price does not rise when staff or volunteers join the account. Clearscope's pricing page does not mention a non-profit discount.

## How does MarketMuse compare to Frase?

MarketMuse is a planning and research tool more than a writing and publishing tool: it builds an inventory of your site, scores your authority on topics, and produces content briefs and strategy documents. Its pricing page lists a Free plan, while Frase, Surfer and Clearscope offer trials instead. MarketMuse does not publish prices for its paid plans; each has a "Book a demo" button.

MarketMuse content briefs give writers "the structure, related topics, questions to answer and linking suggestions they need". There are nine brief types: Article, Comparison, FAQ Collection, Guide, How-to, Listicle, Local, News/Event and Product Review. Optimize and Research include Article briefs, and Strategy includes all 9 types. Briefs can be exported to Google Docs or MS Word. MarketMuse says it uses "patented topic modeling technology" and that its AI analyzes hundreds to thousands of pages to build topic models.

MarketMuse describes who each tier is for. Free is "Best for individuals with a low content volume" and includes 1 user and 10 queries a month, but no content briefs. Optimize is "Best for individuals publishing content regularly", with 1 user, 100 tracked topics, 5 content briefs and 1 strategy document a month. Research, for "teams looking to add rocket fuel to their content output", has 3 users, 1000 tracked topics and 10 briefs a month. Strategy, for "large teams and agencies scaling their content planning", has 5 users, 10K tracked topics and 20 briefs a month.

MarketMuse also gives personalized metrics. Personalized Difficulty, on a scale from 1 to 100, estimates how hard it is for your site to rank for a topic based on your existing coverage and performance, and Topic Authority considers how broadly and how well you already cover a topic. The home page says MarketMuse finds "quick wins based on your existing authority" and gives "a personalized roadmap showing what to create or update". For a non-profit with an established library of articles, that can show which pages to update first. MarketMuse says it is not a CMS and that it does "not write content" for customers, although its Optimize application has a generative AI component.

### What should you know about MarketMuse?

- Free plan: 1 user and 10 queries a month, with no content briefs.
- Paid plan prices are not published; each plan has a "Book a demo" button.
- Content briefs start on Optimize, with all 9 brief types on Strategy.
- Site inventory, Personalized Difficulty and Topic Authority metrics.
- Briefs export to Google Docs or MS Word; MarketMuse does not replace a CMS.

## Is Surfer a better fit for non-profits than Frase?

It depends on the plan you need. Surfer's cheapest content plan, Discovery, costs 49 USD per month billed yearly for 1 team seat, compared with $39 per month billed yearly for Frase Starter. Surfer's plan comparison shows no AI prompt tracking, keyword research or integrations on Discovery; those start on Standard, at 99 USD per month billed yearly with 3 team seats.

Surfer's core is its Content Editor. Surfer describes its Content Score as "a real-time responsive metric" based on "500+ ranking factors", and the editor compares your draft with Organic Competitors: pages that rank in the top 10 of Google for your chosen location, "as well as top-cited pages in AI answers". Auto-Optimize suggests relevant terms and missing sections, and Surfy is an AI writing assistant that helps you edit and rephrase text.

Surfer's WordPress and Google Docs integrations start on Standard, Contentful is added on Pro, and the Peace of Mind plan adds Zapier and API access. The Content Editor's guidelines can be overlaid in Google Docs through a Chrome extension, or in WordPress through Surfer's WordPress plugin. Surfer's help center describes a 7-day trial with full access to the Pro plan: you enter billing details at checkout, and the subscription converts to the plan you chose unless you cancel within the trial.

For AI visibility, Standard tracks 25 AI prompts across ChatGPT, refreshed weekly, and Pro tracks 50 prompts across all models, refreshed daily. Surfer also sells a separate AI Search Analytics plan at 82 USD per month billed yearly, with 50 prompts refreshed daily and 5 team seats, which focuses on AI visibility tracking rather than writing. Keyword Research is included from Standard, a Topical Map from Pro, and the SERP Analyzer on Peace of Mind.

### What are Surfer's main plans?

- Discovery: 49 USD per month billed yearly, 1 team seat, 120 documents and 10 tracked pages.
- Standard: 99 USD per month billed yearly, 3 team seats, 360 documents and 25 AI prompts refreshed weekly.
- Pro: 182 USD per month billed yearly, 5 team seats and 50 AI prompts refreshed daily.
- Peace of Mind: 299 USD per month billed yearly, 10 team seats, 100 AI prompts refreshed daily and API access.
- Annual billing is charged upfront for the year and saves up to 17%, according to Surfer's FAQ.

## What are the limitations of Frase for non-profits?

Frase's limits for a small non-profit come mostly from plan size. Starter includes 1 seat, 1 site, 10 articles and 50 audit pages a month, and holds at those limits until the cycle resets or you upgrade. A team that needs more than one login must move to Professional, at $103 per month billed yearly with 3 seats; extra seats cost $29 per month.

Frase's pricing page does not mention a non-profit or education discount, and neither do the pricing pages of Surfer, Clearscope or MarketMuse. If a discount matters, ask each vendor's sales team before you commit. AI tracking also grows with the plan: Starter's AI Visibility covers ChatGPT and Google AI, Perplexity is added on Professional, and Claude and Gemini only on Scale.

The trial is short. Frase's free trial lasts 7 days and includes Professional features, capped at 5 articles, 50 audit pages, 25 AI prompts and up to 3 seats. Frase itself says that "No tool ranks you inside a trial", and that rankings and AI citations build over the weeks after you publish. Use the trial to judge the workflow and the quality of the drafts, not search results.

### What should non-profits check before buying Frase?

- Starter has 1 seat; extra seats need Professional or Scale, at $29 per month each.
- Starter holds at 10 articles and 50 audit pages a month.
- Turning content into channel posts starts on Professional.
- Tracking Perplexity, Claude and Gemini needs Professional or Scale.
- No non-profit discount is mentioned on the pricing page.

## Which tool offers the most value for a non-profit budget?

There is no single answer, because the four tools price different things. On published entry prices, Frase Starter ($39 per month billed yearly) and Surfer Discovery (49 USD per month billed yearly) cost the least, Clearscope Essentials ($129 per month) costs the most, and MarketMuse has a Free plan but publishes no paid prices.

Seat count changes the picture. Frase Starter and Surfer Discovery each include one seat, so a team of three would compare Frase Professional ($103 per month billed yearly, 3 seats) with Surfer Standard (99 USD per month billed yearly, 3 team seats). Clearscope includes unlimited users on every plan, which can make Essentials easier to justify where many staff members or volunteers write.

What you need to track also matters. If AI visibility is the main goal, compare the entry plans: Frase Starter tracks ChatGPT and Google AI, Surfer Discovery includes no AI prompt tracking, Clearscope includes Query Tracking across ChatGPT and Gemini on every plan, and MarketMuse's pricing page lists no AI visibility tracking. If planning is the main goal, MarketMuse's Free plan lets one person try its research tools at no cost.

A practical approach is to write down how many people will log in, how many articles you publish each month, and where your writers work, such as WordPress, Google Docs or Microsoft Word. Then use the trials one at a time on the same article, so you can compare drafts and recommendations side by side. Note that Surfer's trial needs billing details and converts to a paid plan unless you cancel, while Frase's trial needs no credit card.

### How do the entry plans compare on cost?

- Frase Starter: $39 per month billed yearly or $49 month to month, 1 seat.
- Surfer Discovery: 49 USD per month billed yearly, 1 team seat.
- Clearscope Essentials: $129 per month, unlimited users.
- MarketMuse Free: no charge, 1 user, 10 queries a month.
- Non-profit discounts: none mentioned on any of the four pricing pages.

## How do these tools compare for social media marketers?

None of the four is a social media management tool. They are built to plan, write and optimize web content such as articles and landing pages, and none of their pricing pages lists an integration with a social network. The closest feature is on Frase's Professional plan, which can turn one piece of content into posts for every channel.

These tools are still useful to social media marketers for the pages their posts link to. If a campaign post sends people to a donation page, an event page or an article, that page is where Surfer's Content Editor, Clearscope's grading and Frase's optimization apply, and their AI visibility features show whether assistants such as ChatGPT mention your organization.

Clearscope suits teams that write in Google Docs or Microsoft Word and want many people on one account. MarketMuse suits teams that need to decide which topics to cover across a large site, with briefs that writers can follow. Surfer suits writers who want real-time guidance as they write, with a Google Docs or WordPress overlay from the Standard plan.

Frase suits small teams that want one tool to research, draft, publish and monitor, and that may want to turn articles into channel posts from the Professional plan. Whichever you choose, schedule and publish social posts in the tool you already use for that, and use these platforms for the content behind the posts.

### What does each tool offer social media teams?

- Frase: posts for every channel from one piece of content, on Professional and above.
- Surfer: real-time guidance for articles in its own editor, Google Docs or WordPress.
- Clearscope: content grading in Google Docs, Microsoft Word and WordPress.
- MarketMuse: briefs and topic research for the content behind a campaign.
- None of the four lists a direct integration with a social network.

## What should non-profits prioritize when choosing an AI visibility tool?

Start with the basics that decide whether people find you at all, then choose the tool. Google says there are "no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary": to be shown as a supporting link, a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet. Google also says you don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files or markup to appear in these features. A tool helps you write and track content, but it cannot replace pages that are indexed and answer real questions.

Next, check which free programs your organization qualifies for. Google for Nonprofits offers eligible organizations "no cost or discounted Google products", and through Google Ad Grants "each qualifying nonprofit has access to up to $10,000 per month in search ads shown on Google.com". To be eligible, an organization must be registered as a charitable organization in a country where Google for Nonprofits is available. Governmental entities, hospitals and healthcare organizations, and schools and universities are not eligible.

Then match the tool to how your team works: the number of people who will log in, where they write, how many articles you publish each month, and which AI assistants you want to track. The comparison table above shows how the four tools differ on each point. A tool with unlimited users, such as Clearscope, suits an organization with many occasional writers. A tool with publishing built in, such as Frase, suits a small team that wants fewer steps between draft and live page.

Finally, check support and training. Clearscope includes free training and onboarding and priority support in every plan. Surfer's plan comparison lists customer support (24h, Mon–Fri), a knowledge base, Surfer Academy and masterclasses. Frase's site links to a help center and a blog, and its Enterprise plan adds a dedicated account manager. MarketMuse links to a knowledge base, webinars and MarketMuse Academy.

### What should be on a non-profit's checklist?

- Make sure key pages are indexed and can show a snippet in Google Search.
- Check eligibility for Google for Nonprofits and Google Ad Grants.
- Count the seats you need and compare plans at that seat count.
- Confirm which editor or CMS each tool integrates with.
- Ask each vendor about non-profit pricing, since none publishes one.

None of their pricing pages mentioned a non-profit discount when checked in October 2026, so ask each vendor directly. Separately, eligible charities can apply to Google for Nonprofits, which includes Google Ad Grants.

### What features should non-profits look for in an AI SEO tool?

Look at the number of seats on each plan, the editors and CMSs it integrates with, monthly article or document limits, which AI assistants it tracks, and the trial terms. Your pages also need to be indexed and eligible for a snippet in Google Search to appear in Google's AI features.

### Can MarketMuse work for small teams?

Yes. MarketMuse describes its Free plan as "Best for individuals with a low content volume" and Optimize as "Best for individuals publishing content regularly", and Research includes 3 users. Paid prices are not published, so you need to book a demo to get a quote.
