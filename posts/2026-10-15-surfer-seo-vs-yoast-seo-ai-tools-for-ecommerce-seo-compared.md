# Surfer SEO vs Yoast SEO: AI Tools for Ecommerce SEO Compared

Online stores now have two jobs in search: getting product and category pages found in Google, and getting products named when shoppers ask an AI assistant what to buy. Surfer (often called Surfer SEO) and Yoast SEO both sell AI-assisted tools for this, but they work in different places. Surfer is a separate web platform for researching, writing and tracking content, while Yoast SEO runs inside the store itself. This comparison describes both using only the vendors' own pages and help documents, checked in October 2026, together with Google's and OpenAI's documentation on what search engines and AI assistants ask of a store.

A note on who is writing: williamreviews.com is the personal site of William Gyltman, who is co-founder and CMO of Rankad.ai. Rankad.ai now operates as Citera and sells AI search visibility software that overlaps with parts of both tools compared here. For that reason Citera is left out of the comparison table and is described in a separate, clearly labelled section near the end, using only its own website.

### What does Surfer SEO offer online stores?

Surfer has a dedicated eCommerce solution page that presents it as a way to optimize product and category pages so they rank on Google and get recommended in AI answers. Its tools include the Content Editor, which gives real-time optimization guidelines while you write, along with Keyword Research, a Topical Map, Content Audit and AI visibility tracking. The eCommerce page lists these store-specific features:

- Optimization of product descriptions, titles and metadata, and of category pages.
- eCommerce templates for product descriptions, buying guides and product comparisons.
- AI-assisted batch content creation and site-wide updates, with brand knowledge to keep copy in your voice.
- Automated internal linking.
- Alerts when rankings drop or AI answers change, and tracking of which AI answers mention your products or name competitors instead.
- Reporting on keyword rankings, AI mentions, traffic and conversions.

The measuring stick behind the editor is Surfer's Content Score, a rating from 0 to 100. Surfer's help center says the Content Editor compares a draft with the top 20 pages ranking for the target query. The score combines an SEO Score with an AI Search Score. Factors Surfer lists include the use of main and partial keywords and NLP terms (and where they appear), the structure of the title, H1 and headers, content length, and images with alt text; the AI Search Score looks at how fully the page covers the facts AI answers tend to include and how early it answers the reader's main question. Keyword Research groups related keywords into clusters, each with a main topic keyword, a search intent and a Content Score benchmark, which is useful when planning category copy and buying guides.

### What does Yoast SEO offer online stores?

Yoast sells two ecommerce products. Yoast SEO for Shopify is an app that works inside the Shopify admin, and Yoast WooCommerce SEO is a plan for WooCommerce stores on WordPress. Yoast describes the Shopify app as SEO guidance built for ecommerce that helps you optimize product pages, collections and blog posts. Its listed features include:

- AI-generated SEO and social titles and meta descriptions for products and collections.
- Automatic schema.org structured data in JSON-LD for products, including price, brand and stock status, with star ratings when used with a supported reviews app.
- Real-time SEO and readability feedback while editing products, collections and blog posts.
- Templates that apply SEO rules across the whole catalog.
- Import of SEO data from another SEO app, and generation of an llms.txt file.
- An optional AI+ plan with AI Brand Insights, which scans how ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity describe your brand.

The AI+ plan is the part that overlaps most with Surfer's AI visibility tracking. Yoast says AI Brand Insights shows an AI visibility score, tracks sentiment, compares you with competitors and shows which sources AI cites when it talks about you. It is web-based: you open it through your MyYoast account rather than inside the Shopify app.

For WooCommerce, Yoast WooCommerce SEO includes everything in Yoast SEO Premium plus ecommerce features: product schema with price, availability, ratings and identifiers, product sitemaps that leave out cart, checkout and filter pages, canonical controls that stop duplicate URLs from competing, improved breadcrumbs, checks on short descriptions and image alt text, and AI-written titles and meta descriptions for product and category pages.

## How do Surfer SEO and Yoast SEO compare for ecommerce?

Surfer is a content platform that sits outside your store and focuses on what you write and how AI answers mention you, while Yoast is an app or plugin inside the store that focuses on structured data, metadata and on-page checks. Both have ecommerce-specific features and both now offer AI search tracking, so the better fit depends on where your store's gap is rather than on which tool is labelled for ecommerce.

Going by its own documentation, Surfer goes deeper on content: guidelines for each page based on the pages that already rank, keyword clusters, templates for buying guides and product comparisons, and tracking of AI prompts across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode. Its eCommerce and pricing pages do not mention adding structured data to product pages, so that markup would need to come from your store platform, theme or another app.

Yoast works where products are edited. Structured data, canonical URLs, sitemaps and metadata templates are applied by the app or plugin itself, which matters most for large catalogs where fixing pages one by one does not scale. Its content analysis works differently from Surfer's: Yoast describes color-coded tips on keyword placement, structure and clarity while you edit, and does not describe comparing your page with the pages that currently rank.

Neither vendor's pages, nor this article, can tell you which tool will move rankings for a particular store. Both publish case studies and customer quotes, but those are chosen by the vendor. The table below sticks to what each company states about its own product.

## How do Surfer SEO and Yoast SEO prices compare?

Yoast is the cheaper entry point for a single store: Yoast SEO for Shopify costs $19 per month, while Surfer's lowest plan costs $49 per month billed yearly. Surfer's plans scale with how much content you create and how many AI prompts you track, while Yoast prices its Shopify app as a flat monthly subscription with an optional AI+ upgrade.

Surfer's pricing page lists six options, priced in US dollars per month and billed yearly. Discovery costs $49 and covers 120 documents and 10 tracked pages, without AI prompt tracking. AI Search Analytics costs $82 and is aimed at tracking, with 50 AI prompts refreshed daily. Standard costs $99 and includes 360 documents and 25 AI prompts refreshed weekly. Pro costs $182 and adds 50 prompts refreshed daily, 1-click internal linking, templates and custom voices, and a cannibalization report. Peace of Mind costs $299 and adds 100 prompts, documents listed as unlimited (Surfer marks this with an asterisk) and API access. Enterprise is priced on request.

Surfer's trial gives new accounts 7 days of the Pro plan. Its help center explains that you choose a plan and enter card details at checkout, that the trial does not cancel automatically, and that your card is charged for the chosen plan when the 7 days end unless you cancel first. Yoast SEO for Shopify and Yoast SEO AI+ for Shopify both come with a 14-day free trial on the Shopify App Store.

On Shopify, Yoast SEO costs $19 per month or $190 per year, and Yoast SEO AI+ costs $45 per month or $450 per year, according to its App Store listing, which also says charges are billed in USD every 30 days. Yoast WooCommerce SEO is sold as an annual plan at $178.80 per year, excluding VAT, and includes Yoast SEO Premium. Vendors change their prices, so check the pricing page before you buy; the figures here were read in October 2026.

Yoast fits more directly, because it runs inside the store: as an app in the Shopify admin, or as a plugin on the WordPress site that runs WooCommerce. Surfer works alongside the store and connects through its listed integrations, its API or its MCP option.

Surfer's integrations page lists Keyword Surfer (its Chrome extension), Contentful, ChatGPT, Jasper, Zapier, Google Docs and WordPress, where it can publish and optimize drafts. It does not list a Shopify, WooCommerce or Magento integration. API access appears on the Peace of Mind plan, and Surfer's integrations page says the API is otherwise available with an API add-on or an Enterprise plan. Without a listed Shopify integration, copy written in Surfer has to reach a Shopify store some other way, for example by copying it across or through Zapier or the API.

Yoast says its Shopify app works with any Shopify theme, and its App Store listing names apps it works with, including the review apps Judge.me, Loox and Opinew, the translation apps Langify and Weglot, and Semrush. ## What can AI tools do, and not do, for ecommerce SEO?

AI tools can speed up writing and checking, but they do not change the rules search engines apply. Google's documentation says the same SEO best practices apply to its AI features, AI Overviews and AI Mode, and that there are no additional requirements or special optimizations needed to appear in them.

Google is specific on a point that matters when comparing these tools. To be eligible as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode, a page must be indexed and eligible to appear in Google Search with a snippet. Google also says you don't need to create new machine-readable files, AI text files or markup, or add special schema.org structured data, to appear in these features. That is worth knowing when a tool offers llms.txt generation: Google says it is not needed for Google's AI features, and that page speaks only for Google. Clicks from AI Overviews and AI Mode are counted in Search Console's Performance report under the Web search type, so you can measure them with a tool you probably already have.

Generated copy also needs checking. Google's guidance on generative AI content warns that using AI to generate many pages without adding value for users may violate its spam policy on scaled content abuse, and that generative models can produce inaccuracies, so AI-generated content should be fact-checked before it is published. That includes titles, meta descriptions, structured data and image alt text, which are exactly the fields both tools help you generate. For product data, Google says Merchant Center policies require AI-generated title and description attributes to be specified separately and labelled as AI-generated, and AI-generated images to carry IPTC DigitalSourceType TrainedAlgorithmicMedia metadata.

For ChatGPT, OpenAI's crawler documentation says that sites which opt out of OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers, though they can still appear as navigational links, and it recommends allowing OAI-SearchBot in robots.txt if you want your site to appear. GPTBot is a separate setting that indicates whether crawled content may be used to train OpenAI's models, so a store can allow one and block the other. That choice lives in your own robots.txt file, not in a content tool.

### What does the official guidance say?

- Google: no extra requirements or special markup are needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode.
- Google: a page must be indexed and eligible for a snippet to be shown as a supporting link.
- Google: fact-check AI-generated titles, descriptions, structured data and alt text before publishing.
- Google Merchant Center: label AI-generated product titles and descriptions.
- OpenAI: allow OAI-SearchBot in robots.txt to appear in ChatGPT search answers.

## What should a store fix before paying for an AI SEO tool?

Start with the store's own product data and structure, because every tool builds on them. Google's ecommerce documentation sets out a short list of fundamentals that apply whichever tool you choose.

Product structured data comes first. Google distinguishes merchant listings, for pages where customers can buy from you, from product snippets, for pages such as editorial reviews where they can't buy directly. It also recommends return policy and loyalty program markup nested under your Organization markup, and product variant markup if you sell sizes or colors of the same item. On Shopify and WooCommerce this is the job Yoast automates; Surfer's eCommerce and pricing pages do not describe doing it, so a Surfer user would rely on the theme or another app.

Next, share product data with Google directly. Google says structured data isn't required to appear in Search but increases eligibility for product rich results, and that a feed in Google Merchant Center gives more control: feeds can be updated weekly, daily or hourly, the Content API allows immediate updates such as stock levels, and Merchant Center participation is required for the Google Shopping tab. Google says providing both structured data and a Merchant Center feed maximizes your eligibility for its shopping experiences.

Then check the site structure. Google recommends linking from menus to category pages, from categories to sub-categories and on to every product, because Googlebot generally doesn't type searches into a search box and may miss products that can only be reached that way. It asks for standard HTML links rather than JavaScript events for navigation, and suggests linking to best sellers from the home page or other content to show how important they are.

Once these basics are in place, a content or AI visibility tool has something solid to work with. Surfer's guidelines and Yoast's analysis both assume the page can be crawled, indexed and understood.

### Which checks should a store run first?

- Add merchant listing structured data to every product page you sell from.
- Add return policy markup under your Organization markup.
- Upload a product feed to Google Merchant Center if you sell many products or they change often.
- Link every product from a category page, using standard HTML links.
- Check that robots.txt allows Googlebot, and OAI-SearchBot if you want to appear in ChatGPT search.
- Fact-check every AI-written title, description and alt text before publishing.

## Which tool should an online store choose?

It depends on the gap. If your product pages lack structured data, canonical URLs or consistent metadata, Yoast addresses that directly inside Shopify or WooCommerce at a lower monthly price. If the gap is content, such as category copy, buying guides and comparisons that have to compete with pages already ranking, Surfer's editor, keyword clusters and eCommerce templates are built for that work. If you mainly want to know whether AI assistants name your products, both offer tracking: Surfer through its AI Search Analytics plan and its content plans from Standard up, Yoast through AI+. The two are not mutually exclusive, since one handles markup inside the store and the other handles the writing.
